For families living in Japan

Your first junior golf tournament in Japan

Your child wants to play in a tournament, but the pages and entry forms are all in Japanese. This guide walks you through the whole process in order, from choosing a tournament to the day itself.

Each organizer has its own entry method and rules. This page explains what most tournaments have in common, with real examples from 2026 guidelines. Before you enter, always read the guidelines (要項, yōkō) for the tournament: they list the dates, who can enter, fees and how to apply.

Tip: your browser’s translate function works on most official pages. For PDFs, a translation app with a camera mode helps. Numbers in brackets point to the sources at the bottom.

The steps to your first tournament

STEP1

Choose a tournament

Tournaments range from small local events to series where you qualify for a national final. For a first tournament, these three checks help.

  • Area conditions are common. Prefecture tournaments often require the player to live in (在住) or attend school in (在学) that prefecture.
  • Nationality. The guidelines we have checked for major organizers do not mention nationality. That is not a guarantee, so read the guidelines for each tournament.
STEP2

Read the guidelines

Once you have picked a tournament, open the guidelines on the official page, often a PDF. They cover at least these things. Taking notes as you read helps.

Japanese school grades

Japan’s school year starts on April 1, and many tournaments divide players by school grade (学年), decided by date of birth. For the 2026 school year (April 2026 to March 2027):

Other tournaments, such as U.S. Kids Golf, use age divisions. If your child attends an international school and you are not sure which grade applies, ask the organizer before entering.

STEP3

Register for a membership, if needed

For members-only tournaments, register before you enter. Registration can take a few days, so do it well before the deadline.

STEP4

Enter and pay

Each organizer uses its own entry system. JGA events use Golf Genius (an online entry system) [2], the JJGA uses its “My Page” [9], and the JGAI Cup uses Sports Entry (スポーツエントリー) [12]. Some tournaments still take entries by fax [7]. Forms often ask for your child’s name in katakana (フリガナ).

Don’t forget to pay. Some tournaments cancel the entry if payment is not confirmed by the deadline [9, 11]. Payment is often by Japanese bank transfer (銀行振込) or at a convenience store, so check the method and deadline as soon as you enter.

Pairings and tee times (組合せ) are usually posted on the official page before the tournament.

STEP5

Get ready the day before

STEP6

On the day

Check in on time. At the Ryo Ishikawa Cup qualifiers, players check in at least 45 minutes before the tee time and wait near the tee 10 minutes before [9]. At the Reitaku Mizunami Junior, check-in closes 30 minutes before the tee time, and late players are disqualified (失格) [7].

Bad weather. How the tournament handles rain or lightning, such as cancelling or shortening play, is in the guidelines [9]. Check for messages from the organizer on the morning.

STEP7

Check the results

Results are usually posted on the organizer’s official page. On this site, the details of a finished tournament link to the official results. After the first tournament, thinking about the next goal is part of the fun: many tournaments let players who do well in a regional qualifier go on to a final or a national championship.

More to know

From qualifier to final (typical examples)

Kinds of tournaments

Handicap

A handicap shows a player’s level as a number. Under the World Handicap System it is called a Handicap Index. It is often not needed for a first tournament, but it is needed for higher-level events.
